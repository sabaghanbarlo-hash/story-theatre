How the story turns into visuals

You don’t have to do anything: the page reads each sentence and directs it like a side-scrolling game. Words like walked, ran, climbed, jumped, waved, laughed, looked up, fell asleep make the hero do that. Places like forest, village, sea, mountain, snow, desert, castle and night change the world, and things like tree, house, boat, chest, fire, lantern, flowers, birds appear along the path.

To take control, add lines to your script:

# Stickmen (the first one is the hero; color, hat = cap/top/crown and voice are optional)
@character Kaito | color=#1b1b1f, hat=cap, voice=am_michael
@character Mia | color=#d64545, hat=crown, voice=af_bella

[bg: moonlit forest]          (sets the world)
[action: climb]               (next line: walk, run, jump, climb, wave, laugh, point, lookup, surprise, sleep)
[prop: tree, lantern]         (next line: things to show)
Once upon a time, a boy looked up at the moon.
Narrator: Every night he wondered how it would feel to touch it.
Mia: “You can’t catch the moon!”

Without @character lines, one storyteller reads and acts out everything. With them, unnamed lines are read by an off-screen Narrator and characters speak their own lines.

Voices: af_heart, af_bella, af_nicole, af_sarah, bf_emma, am_michael, am_fenrir, am_puck, bm_george, bm_fable.

Everything runs on your own device and costs nothing. Voicing takes a while on phones; a few minutes of story can take a few minutes to prepare.
